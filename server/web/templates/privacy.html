what we keep

plainly, in the order that actually matters to you. no legalese, because there's nothing here that needs hiding in it.

the widget, before you pair it

nothing leaves your machine. no account, no telemetry, no phone-home on launch. your ratings live in a plain data/ratings.json you can open, grep, back up or delete, and cover art is cached as ordinary image files next to it.

once you pair it

the widget sends, for each song you rate: the artist, album and title as your player reported them, your score and its label, your note if you wrote one, when you stamped it, and the trace if listening was switched on. that's the whole payload.

it does not send: what you're listening to when you don't rate it, how long you listened, cover art (the server finds its own from MusicBrainz), or anything about your machine beyond a device name you can rename and revoke.

videos never sync at all

windows tells the widget whether what's playing is music or a video. videos get rated exactly the same way but land in their own file on their own shelf, and the sync engine is never handed them — not as a setting, as a wiring decision.

the account

your email address, a hash of your password (never the password), your handle, and — if you signed in with google or discord — that provider's user id and the address it gave us. avatars are generated from a random seed, so there are no uploads, which means no image moderation and nothing of yours sitting in a bucket.

what's public

verdicts you mark public, on a shelf you haven't made private. a note can be held back on its own while its score stays visible. a private shelf shows strangers nothing but that it exists.

addresses and logs

rate limiting counts requests against your ip for a few minutes at a time so that scripts can't hammer the place. those counters are deleted within the hour and there is no other log of who read what — no analytics, no pixels, no third-party scripts, and the fonts are served from this server rather than a cdn. that's why there's no cookie banner: the only cookie is the one that keeps you signed in.

taking it back

export — /v1/export hands back every verdict we hold on you, in the same shape as the widget's own file.
delete — from settings. every rating goes, and any song nobody else was standing behind goes with it, which is the same rule that created it in the first place.
unpair — revoke a device from settings and its token stops working immediately; the widget notices and keeps your local library.

questions, or something here that doesn't match what you observe: the discord, or an issue on github.
